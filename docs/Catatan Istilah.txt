== Brain Tumor MRI Segmentation

Intracranial malignancies, predominantly gliomas, constitute a major clinical
challenge characterized by substantial morbidity and dismal prognostic outcomes.
Among these, glioblastoma represents the most aggressive primary brain tumor,
exhibiting a 5-year relative survival rate of approximately 6.8% and a median
survival duration of less than one year @jin2025advancements @zhou2026bufnet.
In neuro-oncological management, establishing the maximal safe extent of
resection alongside precise radiotherapy planning is critical for extending
patient survival. Multiparametric Magnetic Resonance Imaging (mpMRI) serves as
the indispensable non-invasive standard for visualizing intracranial neoplasia
due to its superior soft-tissue contrast across diverse physical acquisition
parameters. Routine diagnostic protocols acquire four complementary modalities:
native T1-weighted (T1), contrast-enhanced T1-weighted with gadolinium
(T1c/T1Gd), T2-weighted (T2), and Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR).
Each modality captures distinct physiological and biological properties of the
tumor microenvironment; T1 and T1c sequences highlight disruptions in the
blood-brain barrier and delineate the hyperintense tumor core and necrotic
tissue, whereas T2 and FLAIR sequences selectively accentuate peritumoral
vasogenic edema by suppressing free water signals
@jin2025advancements @zhou2026bufnet @wang2021brain.

To standardize benchmarking and foster algorithmic advances in automated
delineations, the Multimodal Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) challenges were
established and have evolved over successive annual iterations
@menze2014multimodal @bakas2018identifying @baid2021rsna. In the standardized
BraTS annotation schema, intra-tumoral tissues are partitioned into three
biological sub-compartments: necrotic and non-enhancing tumor core, peritumoral
edema, and enhancing tumor. For rigorous clinical and computational
evaluation, these sub-compartments are hierarchically structured into three
nested target regions: Whole Tumor (WT), which encompasses all abnormal tissue
compartments; Tumor Core (TC), which isolates the resectable necrotic core and
active enhancing tumor; and Enhancing Tumor (ET), which represents the active
angiogenic lesion envelope @menze2014multimodal @wang2021brain @xing2025msam.

Accurate volumetric segmentation across this hierarchy poses severe technical
challenges. Glioma sub-regions possess non-Euclidean geometries, arbitrary
spatial configurations, heterogeneous tissue textures, and complex,
infiltrative boundaries. While the boundary between vasogenic edema and healthy
parenchyma exhibits gradual transitions, the internal interface separating
enhancing margins from central necrosis displays subtle intensity contrasts and
severe partial volume artifacts @zhou2026bufnet @xing2025msam. Furthermore,
intrinsic class imbalance presents a formidable obstacle, as active enhancing
tumor and necrotic cores frequently occupy a minute fraction of the total
intracranial volume relative to expansive edema and background tissue
@jin2025advancements @wang2021brain. Consequently, manual delineation by expert
neuroradiologists is not only prohibitively labor-intensive and tedious for
high-resolution 3D volumetric scans, but it is also inherently subject to
considerable inter- and intra-observer variability, underscoring the urgent
necessity for robust, automated segmentation solutions
@jin2025advancements @xing2025msam.

== Supervised Deep Learning for Brain Tumor Segmentation

Over the past decade, supervised deep learning architectures have fundamentally
transformed medical image analysis, establishing automated benchmark
performances on volumetric brain tumor segmentation tasks. The cornerstone of
this revolution is the U-Net architecture @ronneberger2015unet, which combines
an encoder-decoder topology with skip connections to simultaneously capture
high-level semantic context and retain fine-grained spatial representations. In
the domain of volumetric neuroimaging, this design has been extensively modified
into 3D convolutional networks, including 3D U-Net and V-Net variants, to exploit
continuous contextual dependencies across adjacent axial, sagittal, and coronal
planes @jin2025advancements @han2024deep @zhou2026bufnet. The self-configuring
nnU-Net framework @isensee2021nnu further demonstrated that standardized
architectural optimization, adaptive preprocessing, and heuristic hyperparameter
tuning could surpass heavily customized convolutional pipelines across complex
biomedical datasets.

Recognizing the receptive field constraints inherent to standard convolutional
kernels, subsequent investigations have integrated self-attention mechanisms and
Vision Transformers into volumetric segmentation pipelines
@wang2021transbts @jiang2022swinbts @hatamizadeh2021swin @zhou2026bufnet.
Architectures such as TransBTS @wang2021transbts utilize 3D convolutions for
localized volumetric feature extraction and employ transformer blocks to
capture global long-range spatial dependencies. SwinBTS @jiang2022swinbts and
SwinUNETR @hatamizadeh2021swin introduce shifted-window self-attention
mechanisms within hierarchical U-shaped networks, markedly improving
computational efficiency while modeling multi-scale contextual relationships
across heterogeneous intracranial tissue. Concurrently, specialized multi-modal
architectures such as BUFNet @zhou2026bufnet integrate boundary-aware
attention modules and multi-modal feature fusion to enhance the discrimination of
indistinct tumor margins, achieving state-of-the-art results on BraTS
benchmarks.

Nevertheless, the operational success of fully supervised deep learning models
remains strictly dependent on the availability of large, curated datasets
accompanied by exhaustive voxel-level ground truth annotations
@jin2025advancements @han2024deep @lu2024enhancing. In volumetric neuroimaging,
delineating complex 3D glioma boundaries across multiple co-registered MRI
sequences requires specialized neuroanatomical and neuroradiological expertise.
The manual segmentation of a single multi-parametric scan can consume several
hours of expert clinician time @jin2026enhancing @han2024dual. This labor
intensity generates a severe annotation bottleneck, rendering supervised
paradigms difficult to scale across broad clinical settings or institutional data
repositories. When supervised networks are trained with scarce labeled samples,
they exhibit severe susceptibility to empirical distribution shifts,
overfitting, and drastic performance degradation on peripheral and
underrepresented sub-regions such as the enhancing tumor core
@jin2025advancements @guo2026semiswinunetr @ottesen2025semi.

== Semi-Supervised Learning for Medical Image Segmentation

To alleviate the dependence on extensive manual annotations, semi-supervised
learning (SSL) has emerged as an appealing paradigm by exploiting abundant
unannotated imaging scans in conjunction with a small set of labeled volumes
@jin2025advancements @han2024deep @su2024mutual. In semi-supervised medical
image segmentation, methodological designs are predominantly organized around
two foundational frameworks: consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling.

Consistency regularization operates on the smoothness and manifold assumptions,
enforcing invariance in network predictions when identical unlabeled inputs are
subjected to diverse spatial, intensity, or structural perturbations
@han2024deep @guo2026semiswinunetr @ottesen2025semi. The Mean Teacher
framework @tarvainen2017mean establishes an exponential moving average (EMA)
of student network weights to construct a temporally stable teacher model,
minimizing prediction discrepancy across perturbed inputs. Subsequent
developments expand upon this concept by introducing dual networks, interpolation
consistency training, and cross-pseudo supervision (CPS)
@chen2021semi @ottesen2025semi @lu2024enhancing, wherein parallel networks
cross-supervise one another using their respective categorical predictions.

Pseudo-labeling, or self-training, provides an alternative and complementary
mechanism wherein a model trained on available annotated data generates proxy
segmentation targets for unlabeled images, which are subsequently integrated
into the training objective to iteratively guide representation learning
@han2024deep @lu2023uncertainty @su2024mutual @jin2026enhancing. Despite its
conceptual elegance, conventional pseudo-labeling exhibits severe
vulnerabilities when applied to complex medical imaging tasks. During initial
training phases with scarce annotations, the segmentation model inherently
generates inaccurate, noisy pseudo-labels due to insufficient feature
discrimination @lu2024enhancing @su2024mutual @liu2026semi. Incorporating
unverified, noisy pseudo-labels into standard supervised loss functions induces
error propagation and triggers confirmation bias, a detrimental phenomenon where
the network repeatedly reinforces and memorizes its own incorrect predictions
across successive iterations @compete2024 @liu2026semi @su2024mutual.

To mitigate pseudo-label noise, common heuristics incorporate static probability
thresholds to filter out predictions that fall below an empirical confidence
level @compete2024 @liu2026semi @lu2024enhancing. However, applying a uniform
global threshold is fundamentally flawed in volumetric tumor segmentation. High
prediction probabilities often correlate with dominant background voxels or
homogeneous non-tumor brain tissue, whereas clinically critical but difficult
foreground lesions—specifically infiltrative edema boundaries and minute necrotic
pockets—systematically exhibit suppressed confidence values
@su2024mutual @lu2024enhancing @xu2026semi. Consequently, rigid filtering
strategies disproportionately discard informative foreground guidance while
permitting high-confidence false positives to persist, leading to biased
gradient updates and suboptimal convergence @lu2024enhancing @jin2026enhancing.

== Foundation Models and SAM-Med3D

The paradigm of computer vision has witnessed a major transition toward
foundation models, spearheaded by the Segment Anything Model (SAM)
@kirillov2023segment. Trained on the expansive SA-1B dataset comprising over
eleven million natural images and one billion masks, SAM possesses exceptional
zero-shot generalization and flexible promptable segmentation capabilities using
point coordinates, bounding boxes, or coarse masks
@kirillov2023segment @zhang2025semisam. Recognizing the potential of universal
segmentation models, recent efforts have adapted SAM to the biomedical domain to
bridge the profound distribution shift between natural scenes and clinical
acquisitions @ma2024segment @cheng2023sammed2d @wang2024sammed3d @zhang2025semisam.
Prominent among these adaptations is MedSAM @ma2024segment, which fine-tunes
SAM’s mask decoder on over one million 2D biomedical image-mask pairs, and
SAM-Med2D @cheng2023sammed2d, which integrates learnable adapter modules across
natural ViT encoders to capture domain-specific spatial patterns.

Despite these advances, 2D medical foundation models exhibit notable structural
deficiencies when applied to volumetric scans such as brain MRI
@wang2024sammed3d @yi2026clinically @foundationliver2025. Slice-by-slice 2D
inference inherently ignores through-plane anatomical context and volumetric
spatial continuity, resulting in jagged, discontinuous segmented volumes and
false positives along peripheral image slices
@yi2026clinically @foundationliver2025. To address this spatial limitation,
SAM-Med3D @wang2024sammed3d redesigns the original architecture into a fully 3D
framework, incorporating a 3D Swin-ViT image encoder, volumetric prompt encoder,
and 3D mask decoder pre-trained on diverse volumetric modalities. SAM-Med3D
demonstrates improved spatial coherence and volumetric representation capability
with a reduced number of interaction prompts compared to its 2D predecessors
@wang2024sammed3d @yi2026clinically @zhang2025semisam.

Nevertheless, critical barriers hinder the direct deployment of SAM-Med3D for
fully automated brain tumor segmentation. First, SAM-Med3D operates
fundamentally as an interactive, prompt-dependent segmenter; it requires
explicit spatial prompts (e.g., bounding boxes or centroid points) to localize
objects of interest and cannot perform autonomous semantic inference in standard
clinical workflows lacking expert interaction
@wang2024sammed3d @zhang2025semisam @xing2025msam. Second, foundation models
lack native multi-class semantic reasoning; they delineate binary foreground
envelopes conditioned on input prompts rather than categorizing overlapping,
nested biological sub-structures such as whole tumor versus necrotic core
@zhang2025semisam @xing2025msam. Third, when applied to brain neoplasms
characterized by diffuse infiltration, weak tissue contrast, and microvascular
proliferation, generic foundation representations struggle to distinguish fine
structural boundaries without precise, manually curated prompt boundaries
@xing2025msam @researchsam2025. Thus, while foundation models offer rich
anatomical shape priors, their clinical utility in brain tumor segmentation
necessitates systematic integration within specialized frameworks capable of
autonomous prompt generation and semantic reasoning.

== Pseudo-Label Refinement and Reliability-Aware Segmentation

To address the limitations of noisy pseudo-labels without discarding scarce
training signals, recent semi-supervised studies have shifted focus toward
reliability-aware and uncertainty-guided pseudo-label refinement
@lu2023uncertainty @su2024mutual @lu2024enhancing @han2024dual. Rather than
treating all unlabeled predictions as uniformly valid or binary targets,
uncertainty quantification seeks to evaluate the dependability of network
outputs @zhou2026bufnet @lu2023uncertainty. Early uncertainty estimation
strategies leveraged Monte Carlo dropout @sedai2019 or network ensembles to
model predictive variance, but these approaches incur substantial computational
overhead during training. To resolve this inefficiency, UPC @lu2023uncertainty
computes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between student and teacher
outputs within a Mean Teacher architecture, utilizing this divergence as a
continuous voxel-level uncertainty map to dynamically rectify pseudo-label
supervision. Similarly, Lu et al. @lu2024enhancing combine cross-pseudo
supervision with entropy-based filtering and prototype contrastive alignment,
suppressing uncalibrated, overconfident predictions on unlabeled volumes.

Concurrently, feature-level consistency and reliability metrics have been
explored to calibrate pseudo-label quality. Su et al. @su2024mutual introduced
a mutual learning architecture between dual decoders that evaluates prediction
reliability via intra-class feature similarity, dynamically weighting
pseudo-labels according to embedding compactness. In the domain of noisy label
learning, DASU @han2024dual demonstrated that uniform uncertainty estimation
across an entire medical volume overlooks localized anatomical variations,
proposing a selective uncertainty module that segregates trustworthy and
untrustworthy regions to guide selective pixel-wise label correction.
Furthermore, external geometric and topological priors, such as superpixel
grouping @thompson2022pseudo and boundary-aware active contours
@jin2025advancements, have been utilized to reconstruct corrupted pseudo-label
borders.

A crucial finding synthesized from this literature is that pseudo-label quality
is highly heterogeneous and varies drastically across distinct anatomical and
pathological tumor sub-regions
@su2024mutual @lu2024enhancing @zhou2026bufnet @jin2025advancements. In
multi-modal brain tumor MRI, Whole Tumor (WT) typically spans a larger spatial
volume and displays salient hyperintense boundaries on FLAIR sequences,
enabling semi-supervised models to produce relatively stable, low-entropy
pseudo-labels for this outer boundary @zhou2026bufnet @xing2025msam. In stark
contrast, the internal sub-compartments—the Tumor Core (TC) and Enhancing
Tumor (ET)—are structurally delicate, spatially localized, and characterized
by low intensity contrast and infiltrative boundaries
@zhou2026bufnet @wang2021brain. As a consequence, pseudo-labels corresponding
to TC and ET suffer from high aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, yielding
severe boundary fragmentation and false-negative errors
@jin2025advancements @lu2024enhancing @zhou2026bufnet. Applying global
confidence thresholds or uniform consistency losses invariably favors the
easily segmented WT boundary while leaving the fragile TC and ET structures
corrupted or excluded from supervision. This disparity demonstrates that
effective pseudo-label refinement must be explicitly region-aware,
structure-sensitive, and aligned with the hierarchical composition of
intracranial lesions.

== Research Gap and Position of the Proposed Method

A rigorous synthesis of the existing literature reveals a clear trajectory of
methodological developments and their associated bottlenecks:
Fully supervised deep architectures exhibit remarkable segmentation fidelity
on multi-modal benchmarks, yet their real-world applicability is constrained by
the prohibitive clinical burden of acquiring voxel-wise specialist annotations.
Semi-supervised learning addresses this annotation barrier by leveraging
unannotated scans, but traditional self-training and consistency regularization
frameworks remain vulnerable to pseudo-label noise, error propagation, and
confirmation bias. While uncertainty-guided heuristics and confidence filtering
have been introduced to suppress label noise, existing approaches treat all
voxels or regions uniformly, failing to account for the pronounced disparity
in pseudo-label quality across hierarchical tumor sub-regions, where Whole
Tumor pseudo-labels are predominantly reliable, whereas Tumor Core and
Enhancing Tumor pseudo-labels are highly uncertain and noise-corrupted.

Simultaneously, although 3D medical foundation models such as SAM-Med3D
encapsulate rich volumetric shape and structural representations, their
inability to perform prompt-free inference or multi-class semantic reasoning
prevents them from serving as standalone clinical segmenters. Recent hybrid
paradigms, such as SemiSAM+ @zhang2025semisam, demonstrate that foundation models
can serve as generalist teachers to guide specialist segmentation models in a
collaborative manner. However, SemiSAM+ estimates prediction uncertainty
primarily through prompt perturbation variance, lacking explicit region-level
reliability verification and hierarchical refinement mechanisms tailored to
multi-class brain tumors. Conversely, existing uncertainty-driven SSL methods
(e.g., UPC @lu2023uncertainty, CCE @lu2024enhancing, and Mutual Learning
@su2024mutual) operate strictly within conventional convolutional or transformer
backbones, failing to leverage the powerful pre-trained spatial priors available
within modern 3D foundation models. Consequently, the development of a unified
framework that combines multi-region reliability assessment, structure-aware
foundation model refinement, and hierarchical topological dependencies remains an
unaddressed research gap.

To resolve these interconnected limitations, this paper proposes *“Structure-Aware
Semi-Supervised Brain Tumor Segmentation Using Hierarchical Pseudo-Label
Refinement with SAM-Med3D”*. Rather than discarding uncertain pseudo-labels or
blindly trusting foundation model outputs, the proposed framework is designed to
utilize SAM-Med3D as a targeted, structure-aware volumetric refiner guided by an
explicit region-level reliability mechanism. The proposed method establishes
three distinct core components:

1. The framework introduces a *region-level reliability assessment* mechanism
   that evaluates the semantic confidence and structural stability of generated
   pseudo-labels independently across each tumor sub-region (WT, TC, and ET),
   superseding traditional uniform voxel-wise thresholds.

2. The method formulates a *region-adaptive Accept / Refine / Reject strategy*
   leveraging SAM-Med3D. Specifically, pseudo-label sub-regions exhibiting high
   reliability scores are accepted directly to supervise the student network;
   sub-regions with moderate reliability, which typically encompass ambiguous,
   infiltrative margins, are designated for refinement, where structural
   bounding prompts are automatically extracted from the candidate masks to
   prompt SAM-Med3D, leveraging its 3D spatial priors to sharpen boundaries and
   rectify topological inconsistencies; and severely corrupted sub-regions with
   low reliability scores are strictly rejected and masked out from the training
   objective to prevent error accumulation and confirmation bias.

3. The refinement workflow is executed through a *hierarchical refinement
   pathway* reflecting neuro-oncological structural nesting, progressing
   systematically from the global anatomical envelope to localized inner
   lesions ($"WT" -> "TC" -> "ET"$). By constraining internal core and
   enhancing lesion candidates within the geometrically validated whole tumor
   boundary, the framework enforces anatomical plausibility and eliminates
   biologically invalid outlier predictions.

Through this cohesive design, the refined, high-fidelity pseudo-labels generated
by the tripartite foundation-guided pipeline are utilized to train an automated
3D specialist segmentation model (such as a 3D U-Net or SwinUNETR). Once trained,
the specialist network performs fully automated, prompt-free volumetric
segmentation during clinical deployment, eliminating all requirements for
manual interaction while achieving high precision under limited annotation
budgets.
